## Project Overview

This project presents a ransomware risk treatment assessment and management decision analysis based on the 2021 ransomware attack on Ireland's Health Service Executive (HSE).

The assessment applies the lessons from the HSE incident to a fictional organisation, Northbridge, to evaluate cybersecurity risks, compare treatment options and develop a cost-conscious risk treatment strategy.

It considers operational disruption, sensitive data exposure, supplier-related risks and delayed threat detection. It also evaluates the financial implications of cyber insurance and establishes a revised treatment approach following changes in control costs and recovery test results.

## Objectives

- Analyse the ransomware-related risks facing the organisation.
- Assess and prioritise identified risks using likelihood and impact ratings.
- Evaluate risk treatment options, including avoidance, mitigation, transfer and acceptance.
- Develop a risk treatment portfolio within the allocated $100,000 budget.
- Calculate potential financial exposure under the specified cyber insurance terms.
- Reassess treatment decisions following changes in control costs and backup restoration test results.
- Develop a practical 30-day implementation plan.
- Prepare management recommendations and a board-level decision summary.

## Case Study and Risk Assessment

The assessment uses the 2021 HSE ransomware incident as its real-world reference case and applies the lessons to the fictional Northbridge organisation.

The analysis considers four principal risk areas:

1. **Service disruption:** Potential interruption to critical business operations following a ransomware incident.
2. **Sensitive data loss:** Potential unauthorised access, exposure or theft of confidential information.
3. **Supplier-related exposure:** Security risks arising from unnecessary or poorly controlled third-party connections and workflows.
4. **Delayed detection:** The possibility that ransomware activity may remain undetected, increasing the extent of damage.

## Risk Treatment Strategy

The proposed treatment strategy evaluates preventive, detective and recovery-focused controls.

The initial treatment portfolio was developed against the $100,000 budget. Following the revised scenario, the treatment decisions were reassessed to account for the increased cost of multi-factor authentication (MFA) and the unsuccessful backup restoration test.

The revised portfolio prioritises:

- Retirement of the unnecessary supplier workflow.
- Multi-factor authentication and access reviews.
- Protected backups, restoration testing and recovery planning.
- Security awareness and incident reporting improvements.

The managed detection option was deferred under the revised budget, with temporary manual monitoring arrangements and assigned ownership.

No critical service-continuity risk is treated as acceptable without further remediation and successful recovery validation.

## Cyber Insurance Analysis

The assessment evaluates the financial implications of the specified cyber insurance policy using the following terms:

- **Annual premium:** $20,000.
- **Deductible:** $25,000.
- **Insurer payment limit:** $250,000.

The analysis considers covered losses and excluded claims to determine the insurer's potential contribution and the financial exposure retained by the organisation.

It also highlights that cyber insurance does not replace the need for effective security controls and tested recovery arrangements.

## Key Recommendations

- Prioritise MFA and access control improvements across relevant accounts.
- Maintain protected backups and conduct successful restoration tests.
- Retire unnecessary supplier workflows to reduce the attack surface.
- Strengthen employee security awareness and incident reporting.
- Establish temporary manual monitoring and escalation arrangements while managed detection is deferred.
- Assign clear ownership and deadlines to outstanding risk treatment actions.
- Review residual risks regularly and escalate significant changes to management.

## Methodology and Limitations

The assessment applies risk assessment and treatment principles using the scenario information and assumptions provided in the assignment brief.

Risk ratings were determined using likelihood and impact assessments. Financial calculations were based on the stated control costs, budget limits and insurance policy terms.

The HSE incident provides the real-world reference, while Northbridge's organisational circumstances, risk scenarios, financial assumptions and proposed treatment costs are fictional and intended for academic analysis.

The treatment recommendations are scenario-based and would require further validation against an organisation's actual risk appetite, technical environment, business impact analysis and operational requirements before implementation.

## Conclusion

The assessment demonstrates the importance of a structured ransomware risk treatment strategy that combines preventive security controls, effective detection, tested recovery capabilities and informed management decisions.

The revised treatment portfolio reflects the need to adapt risk management decisions when control costs change or when security controls fail validation.

Ultimately, ransomware resilience requires more than purchasing security solutions. It depends on effective governance, clear accountability, continuous monitoring and the ability to restore critical services reliably.
